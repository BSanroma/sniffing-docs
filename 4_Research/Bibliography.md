# 4. Research & Bibliography

The Sniffing methodology does not exist in a vacuum. It is deeply rooted in contemporary challenges identified by scientometrics, knowledge management, and AI research. Below is a summary of the academic context and foundational theories that support the Sniffing approach.

## 1. Information Overload in Science
The historical architecture of scientific storage has been based on passive documentation. However, with the exponential growth of literature (over 2.5 million new articles annually), this model is collapsing. 
- **Scientometrics & Bibliometrics:** Recent studies characterize this as severe "information overload." In many disciplines, the volume of publications doubles every few years, making manual discovery of knowledge gaps or cross-disciplinary innovations impossible.
- **The Limits of Static Repositories:** Traditional databases require proactive, manual querying. Sniffing addresses this by turning the repository into an active ecosystem where documents act as agents searching for collaboration.

## 2. From Keywords to Semantic Vectors
Traditional academic search relies on boolean logic, keywords, and co-citation metrics (e.g., Connected Papers).
- **Semantic Representation:** Sniffing utilizes advanced Natural Language Processing (NLP) and high-dimensional vector embeddings to represent the deep ontological identity and epistemic contributions of a text.
- **Emergent Proximity:** Unlike static clustering, Sniffing calculates distance based on "Needs" and "Contributions." Two papers from different decades and fields may have zero shared keywords but will be pulled together if one solves the other's declared limitation.

## 3. Multi-Agent Systems & Conversational Knowledge
Before physical archives, human knowledge was inherently oral, communal, and conversational. Sniffing aims to restore this natural state using AI.
- **Generative AI in Research:** While LLMs are often used for simple summarization, Sniffing employs them in a Multi-Agent System (MAS). 
- **The Dialogue Table:** Inspired by collaborative problem-solving theories, agents adopt specific roles to debate intersections. This mirrors the peer-review and academic conference environments, transforming inert data back into "living" conversational knowledge.

## Foundational Inspiration
- **Knowledge Graphs:** Methodologies for representing entities and relationships dynamically.
- **Lateral Thinking (De Bono):** The core philosophy of breaking information silos and finding non-obvious solutions by connecting disparate domains.
- **Biomimetics & Ecosystems:** Modeling the database not as a filing cabinet, but as a biological ecosystem where ideas "pollinate" across boundaries.
